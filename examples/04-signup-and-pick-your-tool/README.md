# Pick your tool first, then start installing

> The fork in the road for this course: pick your tool, get your account ready, then each of you goes your own way.

## 1. Overview

The first two articles covered "why learn this" and "what you're actually using." This one is the last stop before you touch anything, and it's the fork in the road for the whole course.

There are really only two things to do here: get your account sorted, and lock in which tool you're going to learn. Once both are done, you only need to read the articles for your own path and can skip the rest.

---

## 2. Learning goals

A lot of people get stuck at this exact step, and what trips them up usually isn't the technology, it's the choice itself: all three tools sound good, so which one do you actually learn? So they end up reading about all three and installing none of them.

This article is here to settle that once and for all. Remember one thing: **you only need to pick one of these three to learn**. Their usage overlaps about ninety percent. Get good at any one of them, and switching to another takes maybe a day or two of adjustment. What actually costs you time is not deciding.

By the end of this task, you'll be able to:

1. Know where to go for account signup and subscriptions, and why this course doesn't teach it.
2. Lock in which of the three tools to learn based on where you live and where your career is headed, and be able to explain why.
3. Build a small habit: whenever you ask a question later, say up front which tool you're using.

## 5. Before you install anything, you need an account

All three tools are free to download and free to install. But as covered in article 03, what you're actually paying for is the brain's compute, so before you install anything you need a working account with a subscription set up.

For readers based in mainland China, the hard part isn't the software, it's a pile of tedious real-world logistics: a North American email address that can receive verification codes, a stable US phone number, a payment method you can openly use, and figuring out which subscription tier to pick for each product.

**This course doesn't teach any of that.** That's not laziness, it's a deliberate division of labor: those problems are detailed, they change constantly, and covering them properly would be a whole separate series on their own. Cramming them into this course would just crowd out everything else.

For that part, go straight to this guide, it covers everything above:

- [US AI Product Signup and Subscription Guide (Personal Edition)](https://offer-forge-public.easyscalecloud.com/projects/offer_forge_blogs/blogs/3pVkdtyRVJpyHsff/index.html)

Get your account sorted using that guide, then come back here. From the next article onward, we'll assume you already have a working account.

Three things to clear up first, so you don't go off track.

**First, we assume you're on a personal account.** This is a general-purpose module, and the people reading it are in all kinds of different situations: some are learning entirely on their own, others are following along with an instructor or a class. But there's one assumption we're making across the board, and it holds true about ninety percent of the time: you're on a **personal** plan, not an **enterprise** plan provided by your company. If your situation is different (say, your company has already set you up with an enterprise account), how you proceed is your call, but let your instructor know so you don't blindly follow the personal-account steps when they don't apply to you.

**Second, you don't need to rush to add a credit card.** The guide linked above walks through the complete process, including adding a card and setting up a subscription. But for this course, the bar for this step is just one thing: **get the account created, get the software installed, and successfully send one message.** The free tier really is stingy, you'll likely burn through it in three to five messages. That's expected, not a sign you did something wrong. Your goal here is just to confirm the whole path works end to end. Once you're actually putting in serious work later and run out of quota, you'll hit that wall naturally, and upgrading your subscription at that point will make obvious sense. No need to do it now.

**Third, at minimum you need your own personal email.** One more suggestion: for most people, getting a ChatGPT account set up is worth doing regardless of which tool you pick, since it's the most broadly useful. And if you end up choosing Codex, it logs in with your ChatGPT account anyway, so you get it for free.

## 6. Which one to pick: go by your situation

Time to decide. The deciding factor isn't which tool is technically the best, it's **where you are and where your path is headed**.

**If you're studying or working in the US long-term, pick Claude Code.** The practical reason: within US companies, Claude currently has the strongest adoption and the best reputation among users. Your future coworkers, interviewers, and team toolchains are most likely built around it. Learning it converts directly into currency in the job market.

**If you're mainly based in mainland China, choose between Codex and Antigravity.** The reason is that Claude has service restrictions in that region, so forcing it to work isn't worth the hassle and doesn't feel reliable. Between the remaining two, as of mid-2026, **Codex is more mature than Antigravity, so it's the one we recommend**. Antigravity launched later and is moving fast, and with Google behind it the potential is real, but it's not as polished yet. If you're already deep into the Google ecosystem, or you just want to bet on the newer entrant, choosing Antigravity is a perfectly reasonable call too.

Worth repeating that principle one more time: **pick the one you'll actually use day to day**, then commit to it. Don't install all three, and don't spend your days second-guessing whether you picked wrong after you've installed one. The cost of picking wrong is much smaller than you think. The real cost is not picking at all.

## 7. Once you've picked, remember to say which one you're using

This section is short, but keep it in mind.

Going forward, whenever you run into a problem and ask for help, whether it's your instructor, a classmate, or a post in a group chat, mention the tool by name. Don't just say "I tried it with an Agent and it didn't work." Say "I'm using the Desktop version of Codex, here's what I did, and here's what happened."

The logic is simple: the interface, terminology, and quirks differ across all three tools, so the same question can have a completely different answer depending on which one you're using. If you don't specify, the other person has to guess, and a wrong guess means a wrong answer, which just wastes more of your own time going back and forth.

Zooming out, this is really an extension of the perspective from article 02: stating things clearly enough that nobody has to guess is itself part of being good at your job. Just keep this in the back of your mind, don't overthink it, and it'll become a habit over time. And by the way, the same principle applies when you're asking an AI, too.

## 9. Exercise

### Exercise: Get your account set up

**Goal:** Clear the one external hurdle in this course. In plain terms: get your account registered, for free.

**How to do it:**

1. Confirm you have your own personal email address.
2. Open the signup and subscription guide linked above, and work through it in order until the account for the tool you picked can log in.
3. Log in once through the web version, send any message, and confirm the account is alive.
4. You can skip adding a card and setting up a subscription for now.

**What you'll notice:**

The free quota tends to run out after about three to five messages, and then you won't be able to send more. Don't panic, that's just how the free tier works, it's not that you broke something. As long as that one message went through successfully, you've cleared this step.

> **Key insight:** Confirm your account is alive before you install the software. Otherwise, if something errors out partway through installation, you won't be able to tell whether it's a software problem or an account problem.
